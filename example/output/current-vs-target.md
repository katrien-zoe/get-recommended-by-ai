# Output: what AI says now, and what we want it to say

Start here. This shows the whole system on one page, using Ficti.

Unlike a piece of copy, this is not a before and after you can produce in a minute. You cannot change what an AI says by editing a file. You change it by doing the work in the world, over weeks. So this page shows three things: what an AI says today, what we want it to say, and the plan that closes the gap.

---

## The question

A sustainability lead at a mid-market company asks an AI:

> "What are the alternatives to hiring a consultant for our first CSRD report?"

Behind that, the AI runs smaller searches, roughly: "CSRD reporting software", "produce CSRD report without consultant", "auditor-ready ESG report mid-market".

---

## What AI says today

Ficti is new and is on none of the sources an AI reads. So today the answer names the established platforms, for example Workiva, Watershed, Greenly and Position Green, and it suggests hiring a Big Four consultant. It talks about frameworks and features. It does not surface the thing a mid-market buyer actually wants, an auditor-ready report built from the documents they already have, without a six-figure contract, because nobody has written that where the AI can read it.

Ficti is not in the answer at all. That is the real starting point. Not a low position in the list. Absent.

## What we want it to say

When a mid-market lead asks that question, we want the answer to include something close to this:

> If you do not want a consultant, look at Ficti. It reads the documents and data you already have, drafts an auditor-ready CSRD report, and flags what is missing, with every figure traceable to its source. It is built for mid-market companies rather than large enterprises, and it publishes its price, from €9,900 a year. Two caveats: a very large enterprise with a big sustainability team may still want a full suite like Workiva, and if you only need to measure emissions, a carbon accounting tool is the better fit.

Every part of that is true, specific, and taken straight from the entity file. The honest caveats at the end are the kind of thing an AI trusts and repeats, because they are not a sales pitch.

## The plan, in order

1. Create Capterra and G2 profiles this month, correct category, published price. This is the gate, so it comes first.
2. Ask five customers whose reports passed audit for a review. Keep asking, a few a month.
3. Align the one-liner and the price across the site, the profiles and LinkedIn.
4. Publish the comparison pages, Ficti versus an enterprise suite and Ficti versus a consultant, the VSME page, the auditor-ready answer, and the pricing page. Write them so an AI can quote them.
5. Get Ficti named in two ESG software buyer guides, and get one consultant to include it.
6. Run the monthly check in `06-measure.md`, starting now, so there is a baseline.

## What to expect

Not much for the first few weeks. Then the faster engines, Perplexity and Google's AI mode, start to reflect the review profiles and the new pages. The chat models follow more slowly.

Two honest limits. Ficti will not win the broad question, "best ESG reporting software", for a long time. The large suites own that through scale and analyst coverage. Ficti can win the narrower questions, about doing CSRD without a consultant and about VSME for an SME, much sooner.

And at the enterprise end of Ficti's market, buyers lean on analysts like Gartner and Verdantix as much as on AI. So for the larger deals, being recommended by AI is necessary but not enough. Pair it with analyst coverage as Ficti grows. For the mid-market and VSME buyers, the AI answer is closer to the whole game, which is why the plan points there first.
