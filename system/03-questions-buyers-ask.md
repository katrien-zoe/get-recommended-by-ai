# Questions buyers ask

The real questions your buyers type into an AI when they are looking for software like yours. These are the answers you are trying to win. They are also the exact prompts you will run every month in `06-measure.md`.

Write them the way a buyer would say them, not the way you would. A buyer does not search "ESG reporting SaaS". They ask "how can we produce a CSRD report without hiring a consultant".

Aim for ten to twenty. Group them so you can see which kind you are winning and which you are not.

---

## Category questions

Broad, top of the search. High volume, hard to win, and you will share the answer with many others.

> - [What is the best [category] for [buyer]?]
> - [Which [category] tools should I look at?]

## Use-case questions

Tied to a specific job or situation. Lower volume, easier to win, and closer to a decision. This is usually where to focus.

> - [What is the best [category] for [specific situation]?]
> - [Which tool handles [specific need]?]

## Comparison questions

Two or more named tools, side by side. Buyers ask these late, close to choosing.

> - [Is [competitor] or [competitor] better for [buyer]?]
> - [How does [you] compare to [competitor]?]

## Alternatives questions

Buyers leaving a named tool. Often your best opening, because they already want to switch.

> - [What are the alternatives to [competitor]?]
> - [What can I use instead of [competitor]?]

## Objection questions

The doubts that decide a sale. Winning these means the AI answers the worry in your favour.

> - [Does [category] handle [common worry]?]
> - [Can I [common concern, for example export my data] with [category]?]
