# Make your pages answerable

Your own pages are read late, but they are fully in your control, and they are where an AI confirms the details it uses. Write them so a machine can lift a clean answer out of them.

The test for every page: could an AI copy one self-contained block from this page and use it as the answer, without reading the rest?

## Structure

- Put a direct answer in the first two sentences, before any story or setup. Say the thing, then explain it.
- Use clear headings that match how buyers ask. If a buyer asks "can I export my data", have a heading that says that.
- Keep headings in order and nested properly. A logical heading structure is easier for an AI to follow than one long flat page.
- Break long questions into their smaller parts, because that is how the AI searches. One page can answer several related sub-questions under their own headings.

## Blocks worth adding

These are the parts an AI lifts most often:

- A short opening answer, two or three sentences, that stands on its own.
- A table. Comparisons, plans, and specifications in a table are easy to quote.
- A real price, as a number. AI often gets software pricing wrong. A clear number helps it get yours right.
- A "who this is for and who it is not for" box. The honest scope makes the whole page more trusted.
- Any statistic with its source named next to it. A number with a named source carries more weight than a bare claim.

## Pages worth having

- A clear product page that states your category, your buyer, and your price, matching `02-your-entity.md`.
- A comparison page for each main competitor, written honestly, including where the competitor is the better choice.
- An alternatives page for buyers leaving a named tool.
- Answers to your objection questions from `03-questions-buyers-ask.md`, each as its own clear section or page.

## Schema

Add structured data where it fits, for example organisation, product, and FAQ markup. It helps machines read the page. Treat it as a small, useful step, not a deciding factor. Do not expect schema alone to change whether you are recommended.

## What to avoid

- A sales pitch before the answer. The AI wants the answer, not the run-up.
- Vague claims a competitor could also make. If it could sit on their page, it does not help yours.
- Walls of text with no headings, tables, or clear answers. Hard to quote means rarely quoted.
